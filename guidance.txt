Issue 01 | 1.1.1 - Non-text Content | Level A | Home | About Us | Footer
Evidence
Alt text failures confirmed across multiple pages and in the site footer.
HOME PAGE - The hero section phone image has alt="Trading App". This is non-descriptive. The image shows a phone running
the WealthFino app and exists to illustrate the product, not convey information. If it is decorative, it should have alt="". If
informative, the alt should describe what the phone is showing.
HOME PAGE - A decorative chart illustration (market analysis image) has been assigned alt="Market Chart". This is a purely
decorative illustration used as a visual aid. It should be marked as decorative with alt="".
ABOUT US PAGE - The Research Analyst profile photo has alt="Krishna Pathak". This image is informational and should describe
the person and their role, not just their name.
FOOTER - App Store badge image has alt="App Store" and Google Play badge has alt="Google Play". Both are functional images
inside links that take users to download the app. The alt text should describe the action, not just the platform name.
NAV DOWNLOAD BUTTON - The Download button in the navigation bar links directly to the Google Play store. The visible text
says only "Download" with no indication of what is being downloaded or for which platform.
Evidence: Auditor observation and axe scan. Screenshots: HOME HERO BANNER WRONG ALT.png | DECORATIVE IMEAGE
GIVEN ALT TRADING CHART.png | RESEARCH ANALYST IMAGE ALT TO BE MORE DESCRIPTIVE.png | DESCRIPTIVE ALT NEEDED
FOR DAPP AND PLAYSTORE IN FOOTER.png | GOOGLE PLAY ALT FOR DOWNLOAD TO BE DESCRIPTIVE.png | DOWNLOAD
BUTTON ALT.png
Fix
The rule: every image needs an alt attribute. Informative images need a meaningful description. Decorative images need alt=""
(empty string — not a missing attribute).
Hero phone image — if this is decorative: <img src="phone6.png" alt="">
If it conveys information about the app: <img src="phone6.png" alt="WealthFino mobile app showing live trade alerts and
portfolio dashboard">
Decorative chart illustration — mark as decorative: <img src="MeetingImg.png" alt="">
Research Analyst profile photo — add a full description: <img src="Imagess/sir.jpeg" alt="Krishna Kumar Pathak, SEBI
Registered Research Analyst and Founder of WealthFino Capital">
App Store badge — describe the action: <img src="app-store.svg" alt="Download WealthFino on the App Store">
Google Play badge — describe the action: <img src="Google_Play_Store_badge_EN.svg" alt="Get WealthFino on Google Play">
Nav Download button — add an aria-label that describes what is being downloaded: <a href="https://play.google.com/..." arialabel="Download WealthFino app on Google Play">Download</a>
After changes, navigate with NVDA. Every informative image must announce a meaningful description. Decorative images
should be silent.

Evidence
Semantic structure and programmatic relationship failures confirmed across multiple pages.
HOME PAGE - The live chart widget brand label is split across two separate heading elements reading "Wealth" and "Fino"
individually. No single programmatic heading conveys the full chart name "WealthFino". The chart widget therefore has no
correctly associated label.
ABOUT US PAGE - Decorative checkbox emoji icons (checkmark symbols) appear before every credential bullet point. These are
not marked with aria-hidden="true". Screen readers announce the word "checkbox" before each item, creating a false
interactive affordance for a purely decorative element.

GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL / INVESTOR CHARTER / TERMS AND CONDITIONS - SEBI SCORES and ODR portal URLs are pasted as raw
hyperlinks (e.g. https://scores.sebi.gov.in) with no descriptive anchor text. The links exist as clickable URLs but convey no
semantic purpose beyond the raw URL string.
COMPLAINTS BOARD PAGE - The data table is coded in HTML with thead and tbody but has no <caption> element and no scope
attributes on any column headers. Screen readers can read the content sequentially but cannot announce the table name or
associate columns with their headers programmatically.
ALL PAGES - Only two ARIA landmarks are present across the site: navigation and contentinfo. No <main> landmark wraps the
primary page content. Screen reader users navigating by landmark cannot jump directly to the main content area.
Evidence: Screenshots: GIVE CHART A HEADING OR A LABEL AND NOT IN TWO PARTS CURRENTLY -WEALTH ,FINO.png | ABOUT
US PAGE CHECKBOXES TO BE DISBALED FOR SCREEN READER.png | SCORES AND ODR LINKS TO BE ANCJHHORED
DESCRIPTIVE.png | TABLE NO SCOPE AND CAPTION AND EMAIL AND PHONE.png | CURRENT LANDMARKS.png | HEADINGS

Fix
Chart heading fix — wrap the full brand name in a single heading element so assistive technology reads it as one label:
<h1>WealthFino</h1> or use aria-label="WealthFino" on the chart container. Do not split the label across two elements.
Decorative checkbox icons — add aria-hidden="true" to every decorative icon element: <span ariahidden="true">&#10003;</span> so screen readers skip them entirely.
Raw URL links on policy pages — replace every raw URL with a descriptive anchor: <a href="https://scores.sebi.gov.in">SEBI
SCORES Complaint Portal</a> and <a href="https://smartodr.in/login">SEBI Smart ODR Portal</a>.
Complaints table — add a <caption> element inside the <table> tag: <table><caption>Trend of Annual Disposal of

Main landmark — wrap the primary content area on every page in a <main> element: <main id="main-content">...</main>.
This gives screen reader users a direct navigation target.

Issue 03 | 2.1.2 - No Keyboard Trap | Level A | Home
Evidence
Keyboard trap confirmed on the Home page.
HOME PAGE - When a user clicks "Get Started" a modal dialog opens with the title "Client Consent Form (KYC)". The modal
cannot be dismissed by pressing the Escape key. Keyboard Tab navigation becomes stuck inside the modal area. No input fields
within the KYC form (PAN Number, Date of Birth, Phone Number, Email ID) are reachable by keyboard Tab. Keyboard-only
users who trigger this modal have no way to close it or interact with the form.
Evidence: Auditor observation. Screenshots: WHEN CLICKED VERIFY THE ERROR MESSAGE ON KYC FORM.png | WHEN PAN IS
ENTEREED ERROR MESSAGE SHOWS.png
Fix
Step 1 — Add Escape key handling so the modal closes when ESC is pressed:
document.addEventListener("keydown", function(e) { if (e.key === "Escape") { closeModal(); } });
The closeModal() function must: (1) hide the modal, (2) remove any overlay, (3) return focus to the element that triggered the
modal.
Step 2 — When the modal opens, move focus into it immediately: document.querySelector("#kyc-modal-close").focus();
Step 3 — Ensure every input field in the KYC form (PAN Number, Date of Birth, Phone Number, Email ID) has tabindex="0" or is
a native <input> element so it enters the natural tab order.
Step 4 — Trap focus inside the modal while it is open. When Tab reaches the last focusable element in the modal, pressing Tab
again should cycle back to the first focusable element inside the modal, not escape to the page behind it.
Step 5 — On modal close, return focus to the element that opened the modal: triggerElement.focus();
After fixing, test by: pressing Tab to open the modal, then pressing ESC to confirm the modal closes and focus returns to the
page. Then re-open, Tab through every field, and confirm all inputs are reachable.

Issue 04 | 2.2.2 - Pause, Stop, Hide | Level A | Home
Evidence
Three separate auto-moving content areas confirmed on the Home page with no pause, stop, or hide mechanism.
HOME PAGE HEADER - A stock ticker scrolls continuously from right to left across the top of the page. No pause or stop button
is provided. Users with attention or vestibular disorders, and those who need more time to read the content, cannot stop this
motion.
HOME PAGE - A TradingView Ticker Tape widget is embedded below the header. It auto-scrolls live stock data continuously. No
pause control is provided by the host page.
HOME PAGE - Under the "WealthFino Mobile App" section, a panel auto-advances between two sub-sections (Daily Market
View and Community Engagement) without user interaction. No pause button is present.
Evidence: Auditor observation. Screenshots: STIOCK TICKER NO PAUSE MECHANISM.png | MARQUU WITH NO PAUSE.png |
NOP PAUSE BUTTON FOR SCROLLING SECTION.png | BUTTONS CONTRATS AND SAME SECTION TO BE NOT AUTOMOVING.png
Fix
The rule: any content that moves, scrolls, or auto-updates and starts automatically must give users a way to pause, stop, or
hide it.
Stock ticker in header — add a visible pause/play button adjacent to the ticker. Example:
<button id="ticker-pause" aria-label="Pause stock ticker" onclick="pauseTicker()">Pause</button>
In your JavaScript, pauseTicker() should halt the scrolling animation (e.g. clearInterval on the animation loop or set animationplay-state: paused on the element). The button label should toggle between "Pause" and "Resume" to reflect current state.
TradingView Ticker Tape widget — check the TradingView widget configuration for a built-in speed or pause option. If none is
available, wrap the widget in a container and add a custom pause button that hides/shows the widget or uses CSS animationplay-state to halt it.
Auto-advancing Mobile App section — either: (a) remove the auto-advance behaviour and let users click to switch tabs
manually, or (b) add a visible Pause button that stops the automatic switching when pressed.
After changes, load the page and confirm you can pause all three moving areas using only keyboard (Tab to the pause button,
press Enter).

Issue 05 | 2.4.1 - Bypass Blocks | Level A | All pages
Evidence
No Skip to Main Content link is present on any tested page of the site.
ALL PAGES - Home, About Us, Blog, Charts, Pricing, Policies, and Contact pages all require keyboard users to Tab through the
full navigation menu on every page visit before reaching any main content. The navigation contains multiple links. Without a
skip link, keyboard and screen reader users face significant friction on every page.
Evidence: Auditor observation. Screenshot: CURRENT LANDMARKS.png
Fix
Add a skip link as the very first focusable element in the HTML of every page template.
HTML — place this as the first element inside <body>:
<a href="#main-content" class="skip-link">Skip to main content</a>
Add the matching id to your main content wrapper:
<main id="main-content">
CSS — make the skip link visible only when focused (add to your stylesheet):
.skip-link { position: absolute; top: -40px; left: 0; background: #1F3864; color: #FFFFFF; padding: 8px 16px; z-index: 9999; textdecoration: none; font-size: 14px; }
.skip-link:focus { top: 0; }

Test: press Tab once on any page. The skip link text should appear at the top of the screen. Press Enter. Focus must jump
directly past the navigation to the main content area.

Issue 06 | 2.4.4 - Link Purpose (In Context) | Level A | Blog | Policies | Footer
Evidence
Link purpose failures confirmed across multiple pages.
BLOG PAGE - Multiple "Read More" buttons appear on blog post cards. All buttons share identical visible label text "Read
More" with no additional context in their accessible name. A screen reader user hearing a list of links hears "Read More, Read
More, Read More" repeatedly and cannot tell which blog post each button leads to. These buttons also currently navigate
nowhere when clicked.
GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL / INVESTOR CHARTER / TERMS AND CONDITIONS - SEBI SCORES and Smart ODR portal links are
displayed as raw URLs in the page body (e.g. https://scores.sebi.gov.in). While these are clickable, the link text provides no
description of where the link goes or what action it performs.
Evidence: Screenshots: mULTIPLE READ MORE PRESENT AND NOT CLICKABLE AND GOES NO WHERE WHEN CLICKED.png |
SCORES AND ODR LINKS TO BE ANCJHHORED DESCRIPTIVE.png | T&c PAGE SEBI LINK TO BEANCHORED DESCRIPTIVE.png |
SCORES LINK INV CHARYTER DESCRIPTIVE.png
Fix
Blog Read More buttons — make each button purpose unique by including the post title in the accessible name. Use aria-label
on each button:
<button aria-label="Read more about Short-Term Stock to Watch: Triveni Turbine">Read More</button>
Alternatively, add visually hidden text inside the button using a span with a screen-reader-only CSS class:
<button>Read More <span class="sr-only">about Short-Term Stock to Watch: Triveni Turbine</span></button>
CSS for screen-reader-only class: .sr-only { position: absolute; width: 1px; height: 1px; overflow: hidden; clip: rect(0,0,0,0);
white-space: nowrap; }
Also fix the broken navigation so each Read More button links to the correct blog post URL.
Raw URL links on policy pages — replace every raw URL with descriptive anchor text:
<a href="https://scores.sebi.gov.in">SEBI SCORES Complaint Portal</a>
<a href="https://smartodr.in/login">SEBI Smart ODR (Online Dispute Resolution) Portal</a>
After changes, use NVDA links list (Insert+F7) on each page and confirm every link has a unique and meaningful label.

Issue 07 | 4.1.1 - Parsing | Level A | All pages
Evidence
HTML parsing errors confirmed via W3C Nu HTML Validator.
HOME PAGE (KYC form area) - A <style> element is used as a direct child of a <form> element. The HTML specification does not
permit <style> elements inside <form> elements. This causes the validator to report a parsing error.
HOME PAGE - The <form id="kyc-form"> element is not correctly closed. The validator reports an unclosed <form> element,
which can cause unpredictable behaviour in how the DOM is constructed.
HOME PAGE - A <style> element appears as a direct child of <body> in a location that follows a closing </div>. Inline style
blocks must be placed in <head>, not scattered through <body>.
HOME PAGE - A heading h4 (computed level 4) follows a heading h2 (computed level 2) skipping h3 entirely. This is a structural
parsing error that also affects heading hierarchy.
Evidence: W3C Nu HTML Validator output. Screenshot: PARSING ISSUE.png
Fix

Step 1 — Move all <style> blocks into the <head> section of each page. Remove any <style> elements that appear inside
<body>, <form>, or other non-permitted parent elements.
Step 2 — Find and correctly close the <form id="kyc-form"> element. Make sure every opening <form> tag has a matching
</form> closing tag in the right position.
Step 3 — Fix the heading skip. After any h2, the next lower heading level must be h3. Change the h4 that follows the h2 to an
h3, or restructure the content so heading levels are not skipped.
Step 4 — Run the W3C Nu HTML Validator (https://validator.w3.org/nu/) on every page after making changes and resolve all
remaining errors.
Step 5 — Rerun axe DevTools after validator fixes and confirm the "Elements must only use supported ARIA attributes" count
has not increased due to new markup issues.

Issue 08 | 4.1.2 - Name, Role, Value | Level A | Home | Charts | About Us
Evidence
Name, role, and value failures confirmed across multiple pages and components.
HOME / CHARTS PAGE - TradingView heatmap widget contains button elements that use aria-checked="false" on elements
without a matching role of checkbox or switch. The aria-checked attribute is only valid on elements with role="checkbox",
role="radio", or role="switch". Using it on plain button elements is invalid ARIA. axe confirms 4 instances of this error:
"Elements must only use supported ARIA attributes".
ABOUT US PAGE - Decorative checkmark emoji icons used before each credential bullet point are not marked with ariahidden="true". Screen readers announce the word "checkbox" before each item, treating the decorative icon as an interactive
element.
HOME PAGE - The KYC Client Consent Form modal dialog has no role="dialog", no aria-modal="true", and no aria-labelledby
pointing to the modal title. Screen readers do not identify it as a dialog when it opens, so users are not informed that a modal
has appeared or that focus has moved.
Evidence: Screenshots: INCORRECT ARIA AS POINTED BY AXE SCAN.png | INCORRECT ARIA ATTRIBUTES USED AS CHECKBOX
USED CURRENTLY.png | CHECKBOX WRONG ATTRIBUTE.png | ABOUT US PAGE CHECKBOXES TO BE DISBALED FOR SCREEN
READER.png | AXESCAN RESLTS.png
Fix
TradingView heatmap aria-checked error — the aria-checked attribute on button elements in the TradingView widget is
injected by the third-party widget script. You have two options:
Option A — If you control the widget configuration, check the TradingView widget documentation for an option to suppress
aria-checked on button elements or to assign a correct role.
Option B — After the widget initialises, use JavaScript to correct the ARIA attributes: document.querySelectorAll('.tradingviewwidget-container button[aria-checked]').forEach(btn => { btn.removeAttribute('aria-checked'); });
Decorative checkmark icons on About Us — add aria-hidden="true" to every decorative icon: <span ariahidden="true">&#10003;</span> so screen readers skip them.
KYC modal dialog — add the correct ARIA attributes to the modal container element:
<div id="kyc-modal" role="dialog" aria-modal="true" aria-labelledby="kyc-modal-title">
Give the modal heading an ID that matches the aria-labelledby value:
<h2 id="kyc-modal-title">Client Consent Form (KYC)</h2>
After changes, test the modal with NVDA. On opening, NVDA should announce the dialog role and the title. Inside the dialog,
Tab should reach all form fields.

Issue 09 | 1.4.3 - Contrast (Minimum) | Level AA | All pages

Evidence
Colour contrast failures confirmed via WCAG Contrast Checker tool and auditor inspection across multiple pages.
HOME PAGE - The contrast checker confirms 24 small-text elements and 7 large-text elements with a contrast ratio of 1:1
(effectively invisible). Additional failures include 2 spans at 1.04, 15 spans at 3.42, and individual elements at 4.16 and 4.23 —
all failing the 4.5:1 minimum for normal text. The "Start Now" and "Get Started" buttons have contrast ratio of 1.05 against the
gradient background.
HOME / CHARTS SECTION - The dark chart widget area shows black text (#000000) on a near-black background (#05070D),
producing a contrast ratio of 1.04:1.
ABOUT US PAGE - The H1 heading "About Krishna Pathak" shows white text on a navy background with contrast ratio of 1.05.
The sub-heading paragraph text has contrast ratio of 1.18.
PRICING PAGE - Yellow feature text on dark card backgrounds has contrast ratio of 1.53. Multiple span elements fail at 2.54 and
2.56.
BLOG PAGE - "Read More" button text has contrast ratio of 1:1 (white text on white background).
POLICIES PAGE - The selected navigation item (active sidebar link, e.g. Investor Charter) uses a blue colour (#60A5FA) on a dark
background (#334155) with a contrast ratio of 4.07 for small text, failing the 4.5:1 minimum.
Evidence: Screenshots: CONTRATS HOME.png | CONTRATS ISSUE HOME.png | CONTRATS HEADINGS HOME.png | CONTRATS
ABOUT US PAGE.png | CONTRATS ABOUT US HEADINGS.png | CONTRAT ISSUE PRICING PAGE.png | CONTRATS ISSUE PRICING
PAGE.png | BUTTONS CONTRATS AND SAME SECTION TO BE NOT AUTOMOVING.png | mULTIPLE READ MORE PRESENT AND
NOT CLICKABLE AND GOES NO WHERE WHEN CLICKED.png | WHEN SELECTED BLUE FIELD HAS CONTRAT ISSUE ON LEFT PAGE
SELECTION AREA.png
Fix
The contrast requirement: 4.5:1 minimum for normal-size text. 3:1 minimum for large text (18pt / 24px regular, or 14pt bold
and above).
Use WebAIM Contrast Checker (https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) to test every foreground and background
colour pairing on the site.
Start Now / Get Started buttons — the button text is currently invisible due to a near-identical foreground and background
colour on the gradient. Either change the button text to a dark colour (e.g. #1F3864 navy) that contrasts against the gradient,
or change the button background to a solid colour that achieves 4.5:1 against the text.
Chart widget text — ensure the text colour inside the dark chart section is white or a light colour that achieves at least 4.5:1
against the dark background. Example: white (#FFFFFF) on #05070D achieves approximately 20:1.
About Us H1 heading — white text on navy background can pass if the navy is dark enough. Check the exact colour. If contrast
is below 4.5:1 for normal text or below 3:1 for large text, darken the background or change the text colour.
Pricing page yellow text — change the yellow text colour to a darker shade that achieves 4.5:1 against the card background.
Dark amber such as #7A5800 on white gives approximately 5.8:1.
Blog Read More buttons — fix the white-on-white colour combination. Apply a contrasting text colour or a background fill to
the button.
Policy sidebar selected item — change the active link colour from #60A5FA to a lighter shade that achieves 4.5:1 against the
#334155 dark background. White (#FFFFFF) on #334155 achieves approximately 9:1.

Issue 10 | 1.4.5 - Images of Text | Level AA | Home
Evidence
Images of text failure confirmed on the Home page.
HOME PAGE - The "Featured In" section displays a scrolling row of media brand logos (CNBC, Investing.com, StockTwits,
MENAFN, Borok Times, The Economic Times, etc.). These logos contain text rendered inside image files. No meaningful alt text
has been confirmed for these logo images. Where text is presented as an image, it cannot be resized with browser text zoom
and cannot be accessed by screen readers without alt text.
Evidence: Screenshot: NOP PAUSE BUTTON FOR SCROLLING SECTION.png

Fix
For each media logo image in the Featured In section, add meaningful alt text that names the organisation:
<img src="cnbc-logo.png" alt="CNBC">
<img src="investing-logo.png" alt="Investing.com">
<img src="stocktwits-logo.png" alt="StockTwits">
<img src="menafn-logo.png" alt="MENAFN">
<img src="economic-times-logo.png" alt="The Economic Times">
Apply the same pattern to all remaining logos, naming the publication or platform shown.
Where logos are genuinely decorative (e.g. a repeated background pattern with no informational content), use alt="".
Best practice recommendation: replace raster logo images with inline SVG versions where available. SVG logos scale cleanly at
any text size and do not become blurry at high zoom levels.

Issue 11 | 1.4.11 - Non-text Contrast | Level AA | Home
Evidence
Non-text contrast failure confirmed on the Home page.
HOME PAGE - The "Start Now" and "Get Started" buttons in the auto-advancing Daily Market View and Community
Engagement cards have contrast ratio of 1.05. The button boundary (the visual edge where the button background meets the
surrounding page gradient) does not achieve the required 3:1 contrast against the adjacent background. Users who cannot
distinguish colours reliably cannot identify where these interactive elements begin and end.
Evidence: Screenshot: BUTTONS CONTRATS AND SAME SECTION TO BE NOT AUTOMOVING.png
Fix
The non-text contrast requirement: interactive UI component boundaries — button borders, button backgrounds, input field
borders, and focus indicators — must achieve at least 3:1 contrast against the adjacent background colour.
For the Start Now and Get Started buttons sitting on a gradient background:
Option A — Add a solid border to each button in a colour that achieves 3:1 against the adjacent background: border: 2px solid
#FFFFFF; (white border on the gradient background) or border: 2px solid #1F3864; (navy border on a light area).
Option B — Change the button background to a solid colour that achieves 3:1 against the gradient. Test the exact gradient
colour at the point where the button sits using the colour picker in browser DevTools, then check contrast against your chosen
button fill at https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/.
In your CSS, target the specific button classes for these cards. Example: .daily-market-btn, .community-btn { border: 2px solid
#FFFFFF; }
After changes, test using axe DevTools or the WebAIM Contrast Checker with the exact button fill and the background colour
directly adjacent to the button edge.

Issue 12 | 3.1.2 - Language of Parts | Level AA | Legal & Risk Disclosure
Evidence
Language of parts failure confirmed on the Legal & Risk Disclosure page.
LEGAL & RISK DISCLOSURE PAGE - The final line of the page content is written in Hindi: a declaration that translates as a
disclaimer of responsibility. The surrounding HTML element has no lang="hi" attribute. The page-level lang attribute is
lang="en", so screen readers will attempt to read the Hindi text using English pronunciation rules, producing unintelligible
output for any user relying on text-to-speech.
Evidence: Screenshot: HINID ATTRICBUTE ON lERGAL DISCLISURE PAGE.png
Fix

Wrap the Hindi text in an element with the correct language attribute so screen readers switch to a Hindi voice engine for that
passage.
Find the element containing the Hindi declaration on the Legal & Risk Disclosure page and add lang="hi":
<p lang="hi">&#x939;&#x92E; &#x915;&#x93F;&#x938;&#x940; &#x92D;&#x940; &#x91A;&#x940;&#x91C;&#x93C;
&#x915;&#x940; &#x91C;&#x93C;&#x93F;&#x92E;&#x94D;&#x92E;&#x947;&#x926;&#x93E;&#x930;&#x940;
&#x928;&#x939;&#x940;&#x902; &#x932;&#x947;&#x924;&#x947;...</p>
If the Hindi text is inside a <div> or <section> that also contains English content, wrap only the Hindi portion in a <span
lang="hi"> or <p lang="hi"> element:
<span lang="hi">Hindi text here</span>
If your CMS or framework uses a rich text editor, look for a language selector option when editing that paragraph. Some
editors allow setting the language of a text block directly.
After making the change, test with NVDA set to use a Hindi voice. The announcement should switch to Hindi pronunciation for
the marked passage and return to English for surrounding content.

Issue 13 | 3.3.4 - Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data) | Level AA | Home
Evidence
Error prevention failure confirmed on the Home page KYC form.
HOME PAGE - The KYC Client Consent Form is a legal financial onboarding form. It collects PAN Number, Date of Birth, Phone
Number, and Email ID for the purpose of client registration with a SEBI Registered Research Analyst. This constitutes a legal and
financial submission. On clicking the final submit action, there is no review step, no confirmation screen, and no opportunity
for the user to verify their entered data before it is processed. The form submits directly and irreversibly with no mechanism
for the user to go back and check or correct their submission.
Evidence: Screenshots: WHEN CLICKED VERIFY THE ERROR MESSAGE ON KYC FORM.png | WHEN PAN IS ENTEREED ERROR
MESSAGE SHOWS.png
Fix
For any legal or financial form submission, users must be given the opportunity to review and correct their data before it is
committed. Implement one of the following approaches:
Option A — Add a confirmation/review step before final submission:
After the user fills in all fields and clicks Submit, show a summary screen listing all the values they entered (PAN, name, date of
birth, phone, email). Add an Edit button that returns them to the form and a Confirm & Submit button that completes the
submission.
Option B — Add a checkbox confirmation before submission:
Add a required checkbox at the bottom of the form: <input type="checkbox" id="confirm" required> <label for="confirm">I
confirm that the information I have provided is accurate and I consent to its use for KYC registration.</label>
Option C — Allow users to correct data after submission:
After submission, send a confirmation email that includes the submitted data and a link to update or cancel the registration
within a defined time window.
Whichever option you choose, also ensure the form is fully keyboard accessible (see Issue 03 — SC 2.1.2) so the review or
confirmation step can be completed without a mouse.

Section C - Advisory Note

Issue 14 | Advisory — Accessibility Statement | Not a WCAG failure | All pages (footer)
Observation

No accessibility statement is present anywhere on the krishnapathak.com website. An accessibility statement is not a WCAG
2.1 success criterion and its absence is not recorded as a failure in this audit. It is however strongly recommended as good
practice and as a commitment to users with disabilities.
An accessibility statement tells users: what the site aims to conform to, what is known not to meet that standard, how users
can request accessible content or report a barrier, and who is responsible for accessibility on the site. Publishing one signals
transparency and good faith to users and regulators alike.
Recommendation
Create an Accessibility Statement page and link to it from the footer of every page on the site. The auditor will provide you
with a ready-to-use HTML template for this page — paste it directly into your site and update the fields marked with your
name and contact details. No development work is required beyond adding the link in the footer.
The statement should include: the WCAG 2.1 Level AA conformance target, a summary of known issues (referencing this
report), a contact method for users to report barriers or request accessible content, and the date the statement was last
reviewed.
